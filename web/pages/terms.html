What Kuree is

Kuree keeps the record of a chit: who is in it, who has paid, late fines and who takes the pot each month. It does not hold, collect or move money. A chit is an arrangement between its members, and Kuree is not a party to it.

Admins

  • You are responsible for what you enter and for keeping it accurate.
  • Only add people's names and numbers when they are happy for you to.
  • Keep your chit's link to the group. Make a new one if it reaches the wrong people.
  • Make sure your chit follows the law where you live, including any sanction and registration it needs. See Chits and the law below.

Chits and the law

In India, chits (also called kuri or chitty) are regulated under the Chit Funds Act, 1982 and each state's chit rules. Under the Act:

  • A chit needs the previous sanction of the State Government and registration with the Registrar of Chits before it starts.
  • An individual organiser (the foreman) can only run chits up to a set total amount, and must give security and follow the Act's other rules.
  • Running a chit without sanction and registration is an offence under the Act.
  • Prize chits and money circulation schemes, where only some members get money back or returns depend on bringing in new members, are banned.

Kuree is a record-keeping app. It does not start, conduct, register or approve chits, it never collects, holds or moves money, and it does not check whether a chit is registered or allowed. Using Kuree does not make a chit legal. The organiser and members are responsible for sanction, registration and following the law wherever they are. Outside India, other rules may apply.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your own chit, ask your state's Registrar of Chits or a lawyer.

The draw

The bottle draw picks one slot with the phone's secure random numbers before the animation starts, and every waiting slot has the same chance. The admin who runs it decides whether to save the result.
